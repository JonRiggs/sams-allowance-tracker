# Product Brief

Status: Working foundation  
Product owner: Jon Riggs  
Initial household pilot: Riggs family

## Vision

Help families teach children practical money stewardship through a parent-guided allowance process that connects work, responsibility, saving, giving, spending, and the tangible experience of being paid.

## Problem

Allowance is often managed through memory, paper, cash, and disconnected notes. Children may not know which work was approved, what has been earned, when payment is due, what remains unpaid, or how money is divided among spending, saving, and giving. Parents need a reliable household process without surrendering governance to the child or turning the application into a bank.

## Primary Users

- A parent or guardian who configures and governs the household allowance process
- A child who sees age-appropriate chores, completion status, earnings, balances, goals, and payment history
- Multiple children in one household, each with their own eligible work and interface

## Product Promise

At any moment, the family should be able to answer:

- What work was completed?
- What is awaiting parent review?
- What has been approved and earned?
- What is owed but not yet due?
- What is due now or overdue?
- What was actually paid, when, and for which work?
- How much is allocated to spending, saving, and giving?

## Initial Product Principles

1. Parent governed, child understandable.
2. Cash first; no movement or custody of real funds.
3. Multiple children are a core model, not an afterthought.
4. Household data is isolated and parent actions are enforced on the server.
5. Money history is traceable; corrections do not silently rewrite history.
6. Collect the minimum information needed about children.
7. Themes support personal connection without changing financial rules.
8. Responsive web first; installable PWA behavior comes after core reliability.
9. The family workflow is the product; a marketing page is secondary.
10. Real use by the Riggs household precedes claims of market readiness.

## Success Evidence

The household can use the application across repeated weekly pay cycles without parallel paper reconstruction, confused pay-period boundaries, unauthorized child actions, or unexplained balance discrepancies. Parents and children can independently understand the status of work and money.

## Initial Constraints

- Existing TypeScript/React/Vinext/Sites/D1/Drizzle foundation is preserved unless evidence supports a change.
- Development occurs in Ubuntu WSL.
- Initial payout method is cash/manual.
- No bank synchronization, card issuance, payment processing, public child profiles, targeted advertising, or branded third-party skins in MVP.

## Privacy Boundary

Public demos and portfolio materials use fictional households and synthetic records. Private family records are not public test data. An outside-family commercial launch requires security and qualified legal/privacy review, including children’s privacy obligations.
